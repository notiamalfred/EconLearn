Interest & Time Preference

Why a dollar today is worth more than a dollar tomorrow.

Basics Why Interest Exists Interest Without Borrowing Examples

Basics

Two perspectives on interest

  • To borrowers, interest is the cost of earlier availability of resources.
  • To lenders, interest is the compensation for deferred availability of resources.

Borrowers choose to obtain something earlier. Lenders choose to obtain something later instead of now.

Positive time preference

People generally prefer the same amount of the same good earlier rather than later, because the value of current consumption > the value of future consumption (of the same amount).

Example — Peter and Susan

Peter lent a comic book to Susan and received a Karina photocard. His book will be back in one week.

  • To Susan, the Karina photocard is the cost of earlier availability of the comic book.
  • To Peter, the Karina photocard is the compensation for deferred availability of the comic book.

Why Interest Exists

The core reason

Earlier consumption is itself an economic good, so people may pay for earlier access to resources.

Which situations may interest exist in?

  1. Barter economy (an economy where money doesn't exist)
  2. Economy without inflation
  3. Economy without scarcity

Answer: (1) and (2). Interest can exist without money and without inflation. But it cannot exist without scarcity — in a world with no scarcity, you could obtain or produce anything without opportunity cost, so there would be nothing to pay for earlier access.

Interest Matters Even Without Borrowing

Two options

  • Option 1: buy it now.
  • Option 2: buy it later.

The price is not the interest. The value of choosing Option 2 is delaying the use of money. The benefit of choosing Option 2 is earning investment income during the time gained.

Worked Example

Lending a comic book

Decision: Peter chooses to lend his comic book to Susan for one week.

Resources forgone: the use of the comic book for one week.

Compensation received: a Karina photocard.

Cost to Susan: the photocard — she pays it to obtain the comic book one week earlier.

Benefit to Peter: the photocard, which compensates him for deferring his use of the book.
