Glossary of Key Terms

Every definition from the notes, in one place.

Scarcity

The situation in which the resources available are not enough to satisfy all people's wants.

Opportunity cost

The value of the highest-valued option forgone at the decision moment.

Full cost

The value of the best alternative uses of all resources (money, time, satisfaction, etc.) necessarily forgone in making a choice. Full cost = explicit cost + implicit cost.

Explicit cost

Money expenditure made as a result of the current choice (e.g. ticket payment).

Implicit cost

The value of the best alternative use of self-owned resources or receipts forgone (earnings, rent, resale value, time).

Positive time preference

People generally prefer the same amount of the same good earlier rather than later, because the value of current consumption exceeds the value of future consumption of the same amount.

Price competition

A type of competition based on users' willingness or ability to pay. The price need not be money.

Non-price competition

A type of competition based on criterion other than willingness or ability to pay — for example age, identity, queueing time, academic results, or a dress code.

Economic good

A good whose quantity is insufficient for all wants. Requires production cost, more is preferred, a price is willing to be paid, and competition arises.

Free good

A good whose quantity is sufficient for all wants. Free-of-charge is not the same as free good.

Private property rights

Exclusive right to use, exclusive right to receive income, and the right to transfer. Clear delineation means it is clear who has each right and what that right allows.

Demand

The set of quantities of a good that consumers are willing and able to buy at all corresponding prices over a period of time, other things being constant.

Quantity demanded (Qd)

The quantity consumers are willing and able to buy at one particular price.

Law of demand

An increase in the price of a good will result in a decrease in its quantity demanded, and vice versa, other things being constant.

Market demand

The combined demand of all consumers in the market, found by horizontal summation.

Relative price

The price of a good expressed in terms of another good. Relative price of X in terms of Y = normal price of X / normal price of Y.

Supply

The set of quantities of a good that producers are willing and able to sell at all corresponding prices over a period of time, other things being constant.

Quantity supplied (Qs)

The quantity sellers are willing and able to sell at one particular price.

Law of supply

An increase in the price of a good will result in an increase in its quantity supplied, and vice versa, other things being constant.

Market equilibrium

A state in which quantity demanded equals quantity supplied and there is no tendency for the price to change. Qd = Qs = Qe = Qtransacted.

Comparative advantage

Producing a good at a lower opportunity cost than another person. Guides who should specialize in what.

Positive statement

A claim about the objective world that is refutable by facts. May be true or false.

Normative statement

A claim involving value judgement (subjective).
