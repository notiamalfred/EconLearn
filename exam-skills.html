Answer Frames

Definition question

Write the definition verbatim:

"The opportunity cost of a choice is the value of the highest-valued option forgone."

Identify-and-explain question

  1. State the choice and the highest-valued available option forgone.
  2. State the value of that option as the cost.
  3. Include the best alternative use of each required resource; exclude only past costs irrecoverable now.

Cost-change frame

Before the change, the cost is the value of ______.

The change affects / does not affect the resources required, the value of this option, or which option is highest-valued.

Therefore, cost increases / decreases / remains unchanged / may or may not change.

Economic good or free good

  1. "It is a good."
  2. Compare quantity available with all wants.
  3. Add relevant supporting features — never infer "free good" from a zero money price.

Three Checks Before You Write

Whenever an economic variable changes, ask

  1. Which option becomes more or less attractive?
  2. Which scarce resource is affected?
  3. What is now the highest-valued alternative forgone?

Before identifying who should produce what

  • Compare opportunity costs, not absolute productivity.
  • Lower opportunity cost = comparative advantage.

Before classifying a statement

  1. Is it about the objective world? → positive.
  2. Does it involve value judgement? → normative.
  3. Is it true by definition or logic? → neither.

Common Traps

Cost traps

  • Benefit trap: a rise in the value of the chosen option does not by itself change its opportunity cost.
  • Ranking trap: a changed preference order or a new option matters only if it changes the highest-valued option forgone.
  • Past-payment trap: only options available at the decision moment can be forgone.

Goods traps

  • Free-of-charge ≠ free good. A free good is defined by whether its quantity is sufficient for all wants, not by its money price.
  • Price is not interest. When you choose Option 2 (buy later), the value is delaying the use of money; the benefit is investment income during the time gained.

Demand traps

  • Want ≠ demand. Demand requires willingness and ability to pay.
  • Movement ≠ shift. Own price moves you along the curve; other factors shift it.
  • Proportion ≠ units. A fall in the relative price of X raises X's share, not necessarily its unit count.

Allocation traps

  • Money is not required for price competition. Exchanging a collectible toy for a ticket still reflects willingness and ability to pay.
  • Paying money does not automatically make a rule price competition. Check what criterion actually selects the winner.
  • One government service does not a planned economy make. Systems are distinguished by who owns most resources and how they are mainly allocated.
